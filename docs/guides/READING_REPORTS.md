# Reading and reviewing research reports

Open **Research → Reports** and select a saved report. Start with the summary,
recent changes and main facts. Numbered source buttons open the claim beside the
saved source passages, publication/capture clocks, limitations and review result.
Exact raw numeric fields remain available inside a disclosure; percentages in
prose are rounded for reading. Download the evidence package for the original
records, full lineage and executable calculation recipe.

An informational brief need not predict a direction or suggest buying. Optional
interpretations, outlooks and conditional suggestions appear only when supplied.
Questions to follow up are research actions, not announcements that something has
already happened. Every report has a research window and capture dates; an older
saved brief is not a current quote.

News recall now adds bounded source-version/event context and possible newer
related documents. Read those originals before deciding whether they refer to
the same event or supersede an earlier plan. A keyword match or model label does
not establish a cancellation. Strict historical queries exclude information
acquired after their cutoff.

## Flag a concern

At the end of a report, expand **Flag an unclear or outdated statement**. Select
the summary or a statement, describe the concern in ordinary language, and save.
Technical expertise is not required. This stores an unverified concern; it does
not rewrite the report or automatically confirm an error.

The maintainer can attach an attributed review receipt and source IDs. An AI
review is labelled as such, not human verification. If a correction is needed,
a new saved, source-reviewed report can be linked after the correction sources
and numeric checks pass. The original stays readable with its dated notice.
Possible issues awaiting more evidence remain open. Receipts and review events
are append-only; cutoff queries exclude reviews recorded later.

## Limits of verification

Source identity, exact quotes, numeric units/values, hashes and recipe replay are
programmatically checked. These checks do not independently prove a publisher's
truth or that the analyst chose the right formula. Source review and independent
model spot-checks are fallible and have a stated scope. Historical similarities
are context, not a success rate, causal explanation or profitable trading rule.
